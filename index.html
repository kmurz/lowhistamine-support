Common questions

Why do the food lists disagree?

Because food histamine is not a fixed property. It depends on ripeness, storage temperature, time since slaughter or harvest, bacterial load and processing. Published measurements of the same vegetable differ several-fold from one study to the next.

So different lists, testing different samples, honestly reach different conclusions. The disagreement is the actual variability of the food. This app shows you where each list stands rather than picking one and pretending it is settled.

What are liberators and DAO blockers?

Three separate things drive symptoms and most lists blur them. Histamine content is what is in the food. Liberators release the histamine already stored in your own mast cells — citrus and strawberries are low in histamine but commonly listed as liberators. The evidence on liberators and DAO blockers is thinner and more contested than the histamine data, and the app says so on every food that carries a flag. DAO blockers inhibit the enzyme that clears histamine, making everything else you ate worse.

Alcohol does all three, which is why it is the most reliable trigger on the diet.

Why does freshness change the number?

Bacteria convert histidine into histamine while food sits, and cooking does not destroy histamine once formed. Reheating leftovers does not help.

Practical rules: buy fish the day you cook it or buy it frozen at sea; cook, cool briefly, then freeze rather than refrigerate; treat anything more than a day old as a different food.

Where do the numbers come from?

The SIGHI (Swiss Interest Group Histamine Intolerance) scale is the spine, shown next to how more lenient and stricter published diets treat the same food. Those two rows are summaries, not any single named list. Every source is linked in the app under More → Field notes.

The number on the dial is a load score derived from those bands, not a laboratory measurement. It is useful for comparing your days against each other.

How long should the elimination phase last?

Four to six weeks, not indefinitely. It is a diagnostic manoeuvre with a real nutritional cost.

If six weeks of genuine adherence changes nothing, the honest conclusion is that histamine is not driving your symptoms. If it does help, the goal is reintroduction — finding your actual ceiling, not staying at the strictest one.

This app is a food-logging tool, not medical advice.

Histamine intolerance is real but poorly defined. There is no validated diagnostic test, DAO blood levels correlate weakly with symptoms, and the published food lists openly contradict each other. The number shown in this app is a load score derived from those lists, not a laboratory measurement.

Histamine symptoms overlap with mast cell activation syndrome, IgE allergy and several other conditions that need proper diagnosis. Get assessed before you restrict.

A strict elimination is meant to last four to six weeks, not indefinitely, and this is the most restrictive of the common elimination diets. Watch vitamin C, fibre and simple energy intake.

Sudden severe symptoms, especially breathing difficulty or throat swelling, are an emergency — not a food-diary problem. This app does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease.
